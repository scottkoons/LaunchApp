## Design Context

### Users

Scott uses Launch throughout his desktop workday to organize tasks, see deadlines, collect reference material, and prepare printed meeting agendas. On the phone, quick notes, dictation through the keyboard, and photo capture come first.

### Brand Personality

Calm, practical, focused. The experience should feel polished and familiar, with clear controls and brief, visible motion.

### Aesthetic Direction

Scott's September 10, 2026 dashboard references now guide all three modes: clean sans-serif headings, rounded outlined controls, thin blue-gray panel borders, and restrained hover/press states. Scott prefers the original Dark palette: charcoal surfaces with warm amber accents and buttons. Keep the newer DM Sans headings and rounded controls. Space retains navy and warm amber; Light uses cool pale surfaces and ink-blue controls. Use the existing locally served DM Sans across the interface to match the references. Preserve the wide, two-column task creation modal and compact task rows. Use the approved Orbit rocket with Launch and Task Organizer. Avoid clip art, excessive empty space, and decorative dashboard elements.

### Design Principles

- Keep frequent actions immediate and in predictable positions.
- Group related controls and maintain clear alignment.
- Make task and reference content easy to scan without unnecessary containers.
- Preserve phone capture, keyboard access, reduced motion, and accessible labels.
- Refine existing workflows without adding complexity.
